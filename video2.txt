Video 2: Small-Dollar Mortgages

Welcome back to this series of explainer videos on the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act. Today we're talking about a quiet provision in the Act that could have a big impact on anyone who owns lower-priced rental properties: small-dollar mortgages.

Here's the problem this solves. If you've ever tried to sell a house priced under $100,000, you know the buyer pool is thin — not necessarily because people don't want the house, but because they can't finance it. Lenders make roughly the same fixed costs on a $80,000 loan as a $400,000 loan, but a fraction of the revenue. So they simply don't write small mortgages. That's pushed the entire sub-$100K market into cash-only territory — which usually means selling to other investors at a discount.

The new law goes after this from two directions. First, FHA is authorized to launch a small-dollar mortgage pilot — that's loans of $100,000 or less on one-to-four-unit primary residences. The pilot can pay lenders incentives to originate these loans, and give buyers direct grants for down payments, closing costs, appraisals, and title insurance. Second, federal regulators are required to study the fee caps and compensation rules that made small loans unprofitable in the first place — that's the groundwork for permanent fixes.

So what does this mean for you as an investor?

Number one: exit liquidity. If you hold C-class or lower-priced rentals, your future buyer might be an owner-occupant with a mortgage instead of another investor with cash. Financed owner-occupants typically pay more than cash investors. That's a direct boost to the value of your lower-priced holdings.

Number two: tenant-to-buyer sales. Some of your current tenants may become qualified buyers for the very homes they're renting. A direct sale to your tenant — no vacancy, no make-ready, no commission in some cases — can be one of the cleanest exits there is.

Number three — and here's the caution: note that these loans are for primary residences only. This doesn't help you finance rental acquisitions. And it's a pilot program with a four-year sunset that HUD may establish — it isn't guaranteed, and the scale is unknown. Watch how it rolls out before you count on it.

The takeaway: for the first time in a long time, the federal government is trying to make cheap homes financeable. If your portfolio lives in that price range, this is worth tracking closely. Next video: free money for fixing up your rentals — seriously. See you then.
