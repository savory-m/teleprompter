Script 3: 5 Mistakes First-Time Landlords Make (and How to Avoid Them)

Most first-time landlord mistakes don't feel like mistakes when you make them. They feel like saving money, or being nice, or keeping things simple.
Then six months later, they cost you thousands.

I'm Mike Savory with Real Property Management Excel. Here are five mistakes I see new landlords make, and how to avoid every one of them.

Mistake 1: Setting the rent based on your mortgage.
A lot of new landlords start with, "My payment is $1,800, so I'll charge $2,000."

But tenants don't care what your mortgage is. They compare your home to every other rental they're looking at.

Price above the market and your home sits empty. Price too low and you leave money on the table every month for the whole lease.

Look at what comparable homes nearby have actually rented for, and start there. And it's very common for landlords to add monthly fees on top of the advertised rent, so be sure to look at the bottom line.

Mistake 2: Not planning for vacancy and repairs.
Your rental won't be occupied 365 days a year. Between tenants, it takes time to turn the home and find the next renter.

One large national data set found Georgia rentals averaged about 33 days on the market. That's more than a full month of rent you won't collect.

Add in the water heater that dies or the AC that quits in July, and you can see why you need cash set aside before you ever need it.

Mistake 3: Choosing a tenant on a gut feeling.
They seemed really nice. They had a great story. So you skipped the background check, or you didn't verify their income.
Friendly isn't the same as qualified.

Write down your screening criteria and apply them the same way to every applicant. That protects your property, and it keeps you compliant with fair housing law.

Mistake 4: Using a free lease from the internet.
A generic lease template might look official. But landlord-tenant law is state law, and regardless of which state you're in, chances are rules have changed in the last couple of years.

An outdated lease can leave out protections you need, or include terms you can't enforce.

Use a lease written for your state, and review it every year.

Mistake 5: Waiting for things to break.
This one sneaks up on people. Nothing's wrong, so nothing gets done.

But a little routine maintenance goes a long way: HVAC tune-ups, gutter cleaning, pest control, dryer vent cleaning. These small jobs help prevent the big, expensive emergency calls.

They also keep tenants happy, and happy tenants renew. Every renewal is one less vacancy.

So, to recap: price to the market, plan for vacancy and repairs, screen consistently, use a state-specific lease, and stay ahead of maintenance.

Do those five things and you'll be ahead of most first-time landlords.
