Video 4: Manufactured Homes and ADUs — New Tools for Adding Units (Title III)

Today, let's talk about the part of the ROAD to Housing Act that changes what you can physically put on a piece of land — and how you pay for it.

Start with what sounds like a technicality but isn't: the law eliminates the requirement that manufactured homes be built on a permanent steel chassis. That rule dates to the 1970s, and it's the reason manufactured homes have always looked and appraised like, well, trailers. Without it, factories can build homes that sit on regular foundations, look like site-built houses, and — this is the key part — may appraise and finance more like site-built houses. Factory construction typically costs significantly less per square foot than site-built. For investors, that could mean a legitimate low-cost path to adding rental inventory, especially on infill lots.

Second: modular housing. HUD is required to review and fix FHA construction financing rules that have shut modular builders out — including the draw schedules that don't match how factories get paid. Expect modular construction to get easier to finance over the next couple of years.

Third — and this is the one most of you can use soonest: FHA Title I home improvement loans now explicitly cover building accessory dwelling units. ADUs. The loan limits were raised substantially and will now adjust annually with an index, and repayment terms can run up to 30 years.

Think about what that means. If you own a rental — or a personal residence — with a decent backyard in a jurisdiction that allows ADUs, there's now a federally insured loan product designed to fund building a second unit on that lot. An ADU is one of the most capital-efficient ways to add a rental door: you already own the land, and in most markets a detached one-bedroom rents remarkably well.

Two cautions. First, local zoning still rules — federal law doesn't force your county to allow ADUs, so check local ordinances first. Second, the new manufactured home standards require HUD rulemaking, which takes time. The chassis-free homes aren't rolling off assembly lines yet.

Action items: find out your jurisdiction's ADU rules, and keep an eye on the new generation of manufactured homes as HUD finalizes standards. The cost-per-door math on both could be compelling. Next video: appraisals and Section 8 — two operational changes that affect deals you're doing right now.
