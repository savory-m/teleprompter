Video 6: The Rest of the Bill in Four Minutes

Last video in the series. We've covered the investor ban, small-dollar mortgages, repair money, manufactured housing, and the appraisal and Section 8 changes. Today: a rapid-fire tour of everything else in the ROAD to Housing Act that's worth a small investor's attention.

Title 5 is program reform. The headline for us: the Rural Housing Service — USDA's housing arm — gets a major modernization, streamlining its loan programs. If you invest in small towns or rural counties, USDA financing for your buyers, and rural rental programs, should get easier to work with. Title V also overhauls federal disaster recovery housing money, aiming to get rebuilding funds moving faster after storms — which matters to anyone holding property in a disaster-prone area, because how fast a community rebuilds determines whether your investment there recovers.

Title 6 covers veterans. Lenders and counselors will now ask a standard military service question so veterans get routed to VA loan benefits they've earned, and VA loan disclosures get clearer. If you're selling a property, remember: VA buyers can finance one hundred percent. Don't let listing agents steer you away from them.

Title 9 is about community banks — making it easier for them to gather deposits, form new banks, and keep lending locally. Here's why you care: community banks are the backbone of small-investor lending. They're who does your portfolio loans, your construction loans, your commercial refis. A healthier community banking sector means more competition for your business, and that's good for your rates and terms.

Titles 7 and 8 are oversight — annual testimony from housing regulators, interagency coordination, and a stack of GAO studies, including several on how all of this affects housing markets. Those studies are what future legislation gets built on, so the data collected over the next few years will shape round two.

And Title 11 — this one surprised people — bans the Federal Reserve from issuing a central bank digital currency without explicit approval from Congress, through 2030. Not directly a housing provision, but it ended up in this bill, and you'll hear about it in the news.

So let's zoom out on the whole law. Three big themes. One: single-family homes are being steered away from Wall Street and toward individuals — and small investors like you were deliberately left untouched. Two: there's a decade-long supply push coming — factory-built homes, ADUs, conversions, small multifamily. Three: the plumbing of our business — appraisals, inspections, small loans, rural programs — is getting real maintenance for the first time in years.
